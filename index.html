This sheet as text

Overprints are read back as the character they make, not as the strikes they were built from: an apostrophe over a period comes back as an exclamation mark, a dead-key accent composes with the letter under it, and an underline leaves its letter alone. Anything else resolves to the topmost impression. The left margin is dropped; indentation is kept. 


      

        
        
      

    

  


  

    
    
Working the machine


    
There is no cursor and no undo. The carriage sits where it sits, and every key
    you hit stamps ink onto the sheet at that exact spot. Everything below follows
    from that one fact.


    
There is no 1 and no 0

    
Typebars were expensive. Use a capital I for one and a capital O
    for zero — that is what typists actually did.


    
There is no ! either

    
Build one: type an apostrophe, press Backspace, then type a period.
    Two strikes, one column, one exclamation mark.


    
Underlining and bold

    
Type a word, Backspace over it, then hold the underscore to draw a
    rule beneath it. For bold, strike the same letter twice with a
    Backspace in between — the second impression sits a hair off the
    first and the letter thickens.


    
Accents

    
The four keys ´ `
    ¨ ¸ are dead keys:
    they print without moving the carriage, so the next letter lands underneath the
    mark. Click them on the keyboard below, or press ` and
    ~.


    
Fixing mistakes

    
Three options, all of them period-correct: strike over the error with
    x x x, back up and overprint the right letter, or switch on the
    correction tape (F2) and retype the wrong character to lift it off the
    page. The half-space key (F3) squeezes a replacement word into a gap
    that is a letter too wide.


    
The bell

    
It rings eight columns before the right margin. That is your cue to finish the
    word and throw the carriage. Past the margin the keys lock — hold the margin
    release (F4) if you really mean it.


    
Moving around

    
Click anywhere on the sheet to walk the carriage there. Arrow keys nudge it one
    column or roll the platen one line.


    
Typewriter art

    
Every character is exactly one column wide and the platen rolls in exact line
    steps, so the sheet is really a 85×66 grid. Overprinting M,
    @ and % gives you dense blacks; periods and apostrophes give
    you greys. That is the whole medium.
